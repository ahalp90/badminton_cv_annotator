# Player feature evaluation

We compared rally rhythm, movement and shot choices with rally win rate across 43 players in 86 selected elite tournament matches. The figures showed weak relationships overall. Typical contacts per rally and time between contacts had almost no linear relationship with win rate (Figure A12). Clear/lift share had the strongest fit, explaining 8.1% of the differences in players' win rates (Figure A14), followed by centre distance at 5.1% (Figure A13). Players with similar feature values often had different win rates, and win rates overlapped across the behavioural groups. The groups therefore did not establish distinct performance levels. The movement plots also did not establish that being closer to centre or recording less extra travel indicated better results.

Follow-up checks suggested positive patterns for centre distance and clear/lift share (Figures A15–A17). Several tests initially met the usual threshold for statistical significance, but none did after adjustment for testing six features. The logistic models also fitted the data only slightly better than a model giving every player the same rally win probability. They accounted for both players sharing a match when estimating uncertainty, but recurring players and opponent strength were not fully accounted for. Coverage was uneven: eleven players appeared in only one match. These results do not establish that the features can predict performance. That would require evaluation on separate matches, with broader and more even player coverage.
