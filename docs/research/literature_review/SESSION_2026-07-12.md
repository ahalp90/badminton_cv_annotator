## Implied direction

Four candidate additions to the evidence table, in rough order of value:

1. **Court coverage as a reachable region** (control-area paper). Extends the
   existing away-from-centre positional row from a single reset point to a 2D
   coverage area. Load-bearing caution from the paper's own data: full-court
   coverage size does NOT track skill (rho 0.06), only coverage near the shuttle
   does (rho 0.40-0.53). So a naive "bigger spread = better" proxy is wrong; the
   proxy has to be shuttle-relative. Cheap (E/B) as a geometric proxy on existing
   tracks; their neural model does not port (doubles, drone, event labels).

2. **Split-step footwork geometry** (split-step paper). Stance width over height,
   split-step position consistency (spread from a player's own median), and
   split-step reaction time. Static geometry is E from RTMPose + homography;
   reaction time is B/X because it needs an opponent-contact timing anchor the
   pipeline lacks. Reaction time did not separate elite from trained juniors in
   the study, so position-consistency and stance width are the better relative
   signals. Evidence is weak (n=3, a duplicated F-statistic), so this is a
   candidate to test, not a validated row.

3. **Shot-selection predictability / deception** (stroke-of-genius paper). How
   well a player masks their next stroke, from a next-stroke prediction model on
   pose + court position + stroke sequence (all pipeline outputs). Novel and
   cheap, but the paper leaves it as untested speculation and it is confounded by
   per-player data volume. Candidate only.

4. **Rally-length distribution by outcome** (tactical-decision paper). Not just
   rally duration but the share of won vs lost rallies in short/medium/long
   bands, plus final-shot landing zone as the second most predictive feature.
   Gated on rally-winner detection, which the pipeline does not have.

The through-line: the cheap-to-extract dimensions the pipeline can already
produce are mostly the ones the evidence table already covers (timing, shot-mix).
The genuinely new dimensions from 2023-2026 are positional (coverage, split-step)
or outcome-linked (predictability, rally-length-by-outcome), and the outcome-linked
ones are all blocked on the same three missing capabilities: rally-winner
detection, serve detection, and forced-vs-unforced judgement. That is consistent
with the gap ranking in `current_dimension_extraction_infra.md`.

Separate from the dimensions themselves, the MultiSenseBadminton dataset hands us
a **method for building skill ground truth**: three coaches score each player 1-7
from video, the scores are averaged and binned into levels, and the paper reports
inter-rater reliability (Fleiss kappa 0.64 clear / 0.75 drive). The project needs
graded A/B/C/D labels to calibrate and validate any of these dimensions, and this
is a tested template for producing them. Borrow the process, not the numbers
(they used three levels, a 1-7 scale, and two beginner strokes off a machine).
That the labels were graded from video at all, with usable agreement, is modest
independent support for the project's premise that skill on these strokes is
judgeable from video.

The VIRD coaching tool adds no correlations but confirms, from expert coaches,
that shot placement (six court zones), winners vs unforced errors, and "moving
the opponent" positioning are the dimensions worth measuring. That is face
validity for the positional and error-profile rows the table lists as gaps.

## Lingering queries and observations

- Every 2023-2026 paper here is elite or professional. None tests a club-level or
  A/B/C/D contrast, so all new dimensions still enter as relative-form candidates,
  not validated club separators. The evidence table's "expected power" column
  stays inferred for them.
- Two of the four new dimensions are gated on rally-winner detection. If that gets
  built, three evidence-table rows (error profile, shot-outcome-by-type,
  rally-length-by-outcome) unlock at once. It is the highest-leverage missing
  piece for this research line, matching the infra doc's ranking.
- The control-area null result (coverage size does not track skill) is a useful
  warning for any positional proxy: measure coverage relative to the shuttle or
  the rally context, not raw court spread.
- Recurrent quality problem across the ML papers (motion-recognition especially):
  headline accuracies that contradict the paper's own confusion matrices, and
  tiny test sets. Treat single-number accuracy claims in this literature as
  unreliable.

Harvested the reference list (59 refs) of "Making the World's Fastest Racket
Sport even Better: A Systematic Review of AI-based Objective Player Performance
Assessment in Badminton" (Research Square preprint `10.21203/rs.3.rs-9105158/v1`,
University of New England). The review PDF itself quarantined on the gate (benign
web-font cmap noise, FontAwesome + Roboto), so it was harvested but not read.

Diffing its 2023-2026 references against what this session processed: the field
is dominated by stroke/action-recognition and pose-classification papers that
duplicate the project pipeline (BST-X, RTMPose), plus wearable-sensor and
teaching-system papers off the video-grading target. The genuinely new
video-assessable-dimension papers were mostly already caught this session. The
review also cites the published journal version of the in-repo "Court to
Conversation" paper (Knowledge-Based Systems 2025, `10.1016/j.knosys.2025.115027`);
that is the OOP-posture source already in the evidence table, now with a citable
venue.

One clear on-target miss surfaced and could not be acquired: "Exploration of
Player Behaviours from Broadcast Badminton Videos" (Computer Graphics Forum 2023,
`10.1111/cgf.14786`). Broadcast video is exactly the project's input, so this is
worth an interlibrary request. No open-access copy exists.
