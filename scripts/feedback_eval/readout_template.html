Magnified — the part of the scale that is in play

Read the distance, not the score. The red line is the wrong-fault floor. On the raw scale the entire distance between "fluent nonsense" and "perfect" is about a tenth of the axis, and no draw across all 543 clips scored below 0.866. Rescaling against bert-score's own baseline widens the gap between the two model-free baselines from 0.018 to 0.105 — roughly sixfold — which is why the A-vs-B comparison should be reported rescaled.

Reference set

Where the gold feedback comes from

The proposal's risk register flagged that no expert assessments exist. ShuttleSet's annotators already recorded, for every rally, which shot ended it and how it was lost — an expert record of the fault, published with the dataset.

ShuttleSet records the outcome — out, or into the net — not the cause. Inferring "no trunk rotation" from "smash hit out" would look rigorous while being a guess, so the templates are keyed to the observed outcome: eight stroke families × two error modes. The fault statement is annotated; only the wording of the correction is ours.

Method

The split this corpus admits

The first seeded 30% split was rejected outright: the players drawn had played every one of their matches against players in training, so the test side came out empty. That is the strict player-disjoint rule meeting a densely-connected tournament corpus.

The player co-occurrence graph has four connected components, so cutting along their boundaries discards nothing — no clip straddles the partition.

Caveat that belongs in the report

Those components are the men's and women's draws, so this split is sex-disjoint as well as player-disjoint. Test performance therefore confounds "unseen player" with "different game". It is the right split for an A-vs-B comparison, where both versions face the same shift, and the wrong basis for any absolute claim.
